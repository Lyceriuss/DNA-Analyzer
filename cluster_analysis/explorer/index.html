Gene Cluster Explorer

Every tab reads the selected person's genome through public research sources: Clusters (with Nutrition), Medicines, Biomarkers and Gene pathway. Home explains how.

A theme gathers every library gene with evidence that it bears on one area of health, grouped by the mechanism it acts through: repair, growth signals, hormones, the supply of building blocks. Evidence comes from Gene Ontology, Reactome, UniProt and GWAS, and each gene shows why it is included. Colours are the selected profile's calls: blue more active, green typical function, red less active. Female fertility is shown for female profiles, male fertility for male ones.

How the cycle works, gene by gene

Polygenic scores. Grip strength and lean body mass use genome-wide scores from the PGS Catalog (about a million variants each), placed by rank among 525 Europeans in 1000 Genomes scored on the same variants. The other measures use the genome-wide significant lead variants of one large GWAS. They show the genes' share of the starting point, not what training can build on it.

Polygenic risk scores. For each cancer, the PGS Catalog score evaluated by the most studies among those built from a GWAS's risk variants in Europeans (cluster_analysis/themes/cancer_scores.py), placed by rank among 525 Europeans in 1000 Genomes scored on the same variants. A score shifts risk modestly: the large inherited risks come from rare variants in genes such as BRCA1, BRCA2 and the Lynch syndrome genes, which a chip mostly can't see. Green is a lower risk than most.

PathwayGenes in this theme

How XY's genes bear on medicines: whether a drug is likely to work as usual, needs a different dose, or carries a higher risk of side effects. Search a medicine by any of its names (paracetamol, Alvedon or N02BE01 all find acetaminophen) to see the dosing guidelines and drug labels that apply, then every study finding with XY's genotype. Each carries an evidence grade, from A+ (guideline-grade) to C (one study or mixed results). Sources: ClinPGx (PharmGKB), CPIC, DPWG and ClinVar. Calls are measured or imputed at high confidence; rarer variants imputation can't rule out read "not detected".

Drug genes

The genes that clear most medicines. Each is read as two star alleles (*1 is the standard version) and turned into a type with the CPIC rules: poor, intermediate, normal or fast. Select a gene to list the medicines it bears on.

Medicines

How XY's genes act on the way the body takes up, carries and converts each nutrient, written out from curated sources. Per nutrient: the net effect of XY's variants on the blood level (PGS Catalog, GWAS Catalog); how the nutrient is taken up and converted (Reactome); and for each gene where XY has a variant, the step it does, what its protein is (UniProt) and what the variant changes: the protein (Ensembl VEP) or how much of it is made (GTEx). Every finding carries an evidence grade from A+ (ClinVar, expert-reviewed) to C (a single study).

Every fact in the explorer comes from the public research databases below, downloaded or looked up by gene name and variant number. The glossary explains the terms the tabs use.

Common lab tests read through XY's genes. Per test: where XY's genes put the value among Europeans (PGS Catalog, GWAS Catalog), the genes known to move the test, and whether XY's own variants move it: some shift the value without the body being affected ("your normal may differ"), others are real and worth following up. Then how the value is made and regulated (Reactome) and what XY's variants change there (Ensembl VEP, GTEx, UniProt). Every finding carries an evidence grade from A+ to C.

XY's genes grouped into body systems: Reactome's top-level pathways, with metabolism split into its main parts. Per system: the genes where XY's variants make the gene less active (red, left) or more active (blue, right), read from UniProt and MaveDB for protein changes and GTEx for how much of the gene is made. The sources say which way a gene works, not whether that is good or bad, so the bars show direction. Select a system to see its genes, the changed genes that work together (STRING) and the ones that reach into other systems.
